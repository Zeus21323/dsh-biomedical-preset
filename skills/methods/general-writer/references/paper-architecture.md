# Paper Architecture

Use this reference to make every section perform a distinct argumentative function.

## Plan Contract

For each section record:

- the reader question it answers;
- the claim IDs it may express;
- required sources and exact BibTeX citation keys;
- tables, figures, equations, or appendices it introduces or interprets;
- its length budget and transition to the next section.

Do not draft a full manuscript while the plan is pending or revised.

## Common Section Functions

- **Title**: name the central construct, relation, method, or synthesis without unsupported novelty.
- **Abstract**: state the problem, evidence/design, bounded main result, contribution, and limitation or scope.
- **Introduction**: establish importance, precise gap, research question, approach, evidenced contributions, and paper map.
- **Background/Related Work/Literature Review**: synthesize prior work around the gap; do not produce an annotated bibliography.
- **Methods/Materials/Research Design/Theory**: define inputs, assumptions, procedures, variables, models, identification, and reproducibility details needed to understand the evidence.
- **Results/Evaluation/Findings**: answer research questions using mapped evidence, without importing untested explanations.
- **Discussion**: interpret results, compare with literature, explain plausible mechanisms as interpretations, and state implications.
- **Limitations/Validity/Ethics**: identify concrete boundaries, risks, alternatives, and what evidence would resolve them.
- **Conclusion**: answer the research question at the supported scope; do not introduce new results.

Adapt headings to field and venue. A review may use Methods, Thematic Synthesis, and Research Gaps; a theoretical paper may use Definitions, Model, Propositions, Proofs, and Implications; a qualitative paper may use Setting, Sampling, Analysis, Findings, and Reflexivity.

## Contribution Test

Keep a contribution only when the evidence map establishes at least one of:

- a demonstrated empirical or qualitative finding;
- a validated method or system artifact;
- a formal theoretical result;
- a bounded synthesis or taxonomy derived from a documented corpus;
- a dataset, benchmark, protocol, or reproducibility asset;
- a design or policy implication directly connected to reported evidence.

Do not use `first`, `novel`, `state of the art`, `proves`, or broad causal language without exact support.

## Paragraph Contract

Give each paragraph one job:

1. topic or claim;
2. evidence or source;
3. interpretation at the supported scope;
4. caveat or transition when needed.

Keep citations adjacent to supported claims. Avoid unsupported throat-clearing, repeated results, and conclusions stronger than the body evidence.
Record citation keys in the outline, not formatted strings such as `(Author, Year)`. During paragraph generation, emit the citation command in the same sentence as the supported claim.
